=== PATTERN OBSERVATIONS for Paper 5 §5.2 ===

1. **Medicaid non-expansion concentration**: 6 of 8 (=75%) HCA-affiliated PSLF-hostile
   surgical-subspec institutions are in non-Medicaid-expansion states (Florida, Texas).
   Compare to baseline US distribution: 10/50 states are Medicaid non-expansion (=20%).
   The HCA-affiliated PSLF-hostile institutional concentration is approximately 3.8× the baseline expectation.

2. **Metropolitan-area concentration**: 7 of 8 (=88%) are in metropolitan-area core (RUCA=1).
   The single Bayonet Pt institution (Pasco, FL) is RUCA=2 (high-commuting metro fringe).
   Consistent with HCA Healthcare's broader urban-suburban geographic strategy.

3. **HPSA designation**: 5 of 8 (=62%) are at institutions in counties with at least partial HPSA designation
   (typically low-income population subset, not whole-county geographic).
   This pattern suggests HCA-affiliated PSLF-hostile surgical training is partially serving
   underserved population subsets within otherwise urban metropolitan areas — a workforce-policy
   nuance: the for-profit-chain PSLF-hostile institutions are not in 'medical deserts' but rather
   provide care to underserved populations within served metropolitan areas.

4. **State-policy implication for post-Trump-EO**: The 75% concentration in non-Medicaid-expansion
   states means that surgical residents trained at HCA-affiliated PSLF-hostile programs face
   compound policy headwinds: (a) PSLF ineligibility for resident debt; (b) state-level reduced
   Medicaid expansion may affect post-residency practice options for residents who want to serve
   PSLF-qualifying employer types (community health centers, public hospitals).
