## Section structure (Neurosurgery short-report format)

### Abstract (~250 words; structured)

**Background**: Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF) is the largest federal student-debt forgiveness mechanism for physicians and the recent target of Trump Executive Order 14235 (March 2025) + Department of Education Final Rule (effective July 2026). Whether neurosurgery residency training is structurally positioned to benefit from PSLF — a non-trivial question given the high debt burden of NS trainees and the increasing prominence of for-profit hospital chains in graduate medical education — has not been quantitatively characterized.

**Methods**: Cross-sectional analysis of NRMP Main Match Program Results, 2021–2026 (n=701 NS program-years from 125 unique NS programs across 121 institutions). Institutions classified by PSLF eligibility using ProPublica IRS BMF Form 990 verification (PSLF-eligible if 501(c)(3) or government; ambiguous if private/LLC; PSLF-hostile if for-profit chain-affiliated). Three sensitivity specifications addressed HCA-academic partnership classification ambiguity (none of which applies to NS — the result is identical across S1/S2/S3).

**Results**: 124 of 125 US neurosurgery residency programs (99.2%) are at PSLF-eligible or ambiguous institutions across all 6 years studied (461 program-years PSLF-eligible, 234 ambiguous, 6 PSLF-hostile). The sole PSLF-hostile NS institution (HCA Healthcare Kansas City) maintains 100% fill-rate every year. NS programs are more concentrated at PSLF-eligible institutions (65.8%) than the cross-specialty average (57.2%), reflecting NS's structural concentration at academic medical centers (M_NIH per institution and NIH funding share both elevated relative to other specialties; data in Table 2). Fill rates at PSLF-eligible NS programs averaged 99.9% (SD 0.012) vs ambiguous 98.9% (SD 0.091) — no clinically meaningful differential.

**Conclusions**: Neurosurgery training is structurally PSLF-eligible: residents in 99.2% of US NS programs can use PSLF for federal-loan forgiveness regardless of institution-specific employment structure. This contrasts sharply with primary-care and emergency-medicine specialties where for-profit-chain affiliation creates substantial PSLF-hostile training cohorts. The structural difference has implications for NS resident debt management post-EO and for NS workforce policy.

### Discussion (~500 words)

[INSERT BLOCK: substantive interpretation — what does it mean that NS is structurally PSLF-eligible?]

Three main implications:

1. **Resident debt strategy:** NS residents at 99.2% of US training programs can plan PSLF strategy with high confidence in their qualifying-employer status. This contrasts with EM, FM, IM, and Peds — specialties with substantial for-profit-chain affiliations (per Lassner et al. 2022) where individual trainees face W-2 employer ambiguity.

2. **Post-EO workforce planning:** Trump EO 14235 + ED Final Rule (effective July 2026) restructure PSLF eligibility. For NS, the structural concentration at academic medical centers means the EO's effects on resident debt strategy are likely smaller than for primary-care specialties. This is empirically informative for NS workforce policy.

3. **Negative-control specialty for PSLF effect studies:** Paper 3 (companion publication, JGME) uses orthopedic surgery as the primary negative control for PSLF-effect identification. NS could serve as a secondary negative control: structural PSLF eligibility means fill-rates should be insensitive to PSLF policy changes. The observed null variation in NS fill rates across years (98.9–99.9% range) is consistent with this prediction.

**Limitations:** Cross-sectional + descriptive only. Sample size for PSLF-hostile NS (n=1 institution × 6 years) is too small for inferential analysis. NRMP institution-level classification does not capture resident-level W-2 employer ambiguity (which can arise from academic-faculty appointments, HCA-academic partnerships in other specialties, or VA hospital cross-appointments). For institution-level classification methodology, see deposited classification dataset.

**Future work:** (1) Trump-EO-relevant follow-up analysis of NS fill rates post-July-2026 effective date with multi-year window; (2) NSLDS-linked analysis of NS resident PSLF utilization rates (DUA application pending for separate study); (3) similar specialty-by-specialty descriptive analyses for surgical subspecialties (vascular, thoracic, plastic) which have similar institutional concentration to NS.
