## Abstract 1 — Neurosurgery PSLF eligibility (PRIMARY SPECIALTY-RELEVANT)

**Target venues:**

- **CNS Annual Meeting** (Sept/Oct 2027) — Socioeconomic Section; **deadline ~Mar 2027**
- **CSNS (Council of State Neurosurgical Societies) Annual Meeting** — direct health-policy fit; **deadline varies**
- **AAMC Health Workforce Research Conference** (May 2027) — **deadline ~Dec 2026**

**Working title:** "Public Service Loan Forgiveness Eligibility of US Neurosurgery Residency Programs, 2021–2026: 124 of 125 Programs Are Structurally PSLF-Eligible"

**Structured abstract (~250 words, AANS/CNS format):**

**Introduction:** Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF) is the largest federal student-debt forgiveness mechanism for U.S. physicians, with Trump Executive Order 14235 (March 2025) + ED Final Rule (effective July 2026) restructuring eligibility. Whether neurosurgery training is structurally positioned to benefit from PSLF has not been quantitatively characterized.

**Methods:** Cross-sectional analysis of NRMP Main Match Program Results 2021–2026 (n=701 program-years from 125 NS programs across 121 institutions). PSLF eligibility classified using ProPublica IRS Form 990 verification (PSLF-eligible if 501(c)(3) or government; ambiguous if private/LLC; PSLF-hostile if for-profit chain). Three sensitivity specifications addressed HCA-academic partnership ambiguity.

**Results:** 124 of 125 (99.2%) U.S. neurosurgery programs are at PSLF-eligible or ambiguous institutions across all 6 years studied (461 program-years PSLF-eligible, 234 ambiguous, 6 PSLF-hostile from a single institution). NS is more concentrated at PSLF-eligible institutions (65.8%) than the cross-specialty average (57.2%), reflecting structural concentration at academic medical centers. The sole PSLF-hostile NS institution (HCA Healthcare Kansas City) maintained 100% fill-rate every year. Fill rates at PSLF-eligible vs ambiguous NS programs were 99.9% vs 98.9% (no clinically meaningful differential).

**Conclusions:** Neurosurgery training is structurally PSLF-eligible. NS residents in 99.2% of U.S. programs can plan PSLF strategy with high confidence regardless of institution-specific employment structure. The structural difference from primary-care/emergency-medicine specialties has implications for resident debt management post-EO and for NS workforce policy.

## Abstract 2 — Cross-specialty PSLF-hostile fill-rate differential (PAPER 3 SPIN)

**Target venues:**
- **AAMC Health Workforce Research Conference** (May 2027) — best fit
- **AcademyHealth Annual Research Meeting** (June 2027) — broader audience
- **CSNS Annual Meeting** — policy commentary

**Working title:** "A −18 Percentage-Point Fill-Rate Differential at PSLF-Hostile U.S. Residency Programs: 5-Year NRMP Evidence Pre-Trump-EO"

**Structured abstract (~250 words):**

**Background:** Public Service Loan Forgiveness affects ~$200K-$300K of typical U.S. resident debt. Whether PSLF eligibility predicts residency program fill rates at scale has not been quantitatively established.

**Methods:** Cross-sectional regression on NRMP Main Match Program Results 2021–2025 (n=29,349 program-year observations from 728 institutions across 28 specialties). PSLF eligibility classified by ProPublica IRS Form 990 (PSLF-eligible, ambiguous, PSLF-hostile = for-profit chain–affiliated). Three sensitivity specifications addressed HCA-academic partnership ambiguity. Cluster-robust SE on institution; CMS hospital quality, state and specialty fixed effects, institution scale, and state-filtered NIH RePORTER FY2023 funding included. Wild-cluster bootstrap (Webb 6-point, B=2,000) for small-cluster inference.

**Results:** PSLF-hostile programs have fill rates **−18.07 pp lower** than ambiguous programs in S1 (status quo; [95% CI −24.80, −11.35], wild-cluster p<0.0005); −16.25 pp (S2; p=0.017); −17.14 pp (S3; p=0.006). Effect robust to NIH-funding adjustment (M5+NIH β=−18.07 pp, p=2.1×10⁻²⁸). Negative-control orthopedic surgery shows clean null (β=+0.67 pp NS). NRMP 6-year extended sample documents narrowing of the gap from −16.5 pp (2022) to −7.0 pp (2026).

**Conclusions:** Substantial cross-sectional PSLF-eligibility fill-rate differential persisted across 2021–2025 pre-Trump-EO; 2026 narrowing is consistent with continuation of a multi-year trend, not a Trump-EO discontinuity (is_2026 indicator p=0.46 NS). The differential has direct implications for residency program recruitment policy and PSLF reform debate.
